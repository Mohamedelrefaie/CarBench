\subsection{Pre-trained versus from-scratch baselines}
\label{sec:from_scratch}

Two of the strongest surrogates in our benchmark, AB-UPT and TripNet, are evaluated from
\emph{publicly released pre-trained checkpoints}. A reviewer correctly noted that, because the
provenance of the pre-training corpora is not fully controlled, one cannot \emph{a priori} rule
out that part of our test geometries were seen during pre-training, which would inflate the
reported accuracy. To isolate this effect we retrain both models \emph{from random
initialization} on the DrivAerNet\texttt{++} training split only, and evaluate them on the
identical held-out test split and metrics used throughout the paper (surface pressure only).

For AB-UPT we reproduce the authors' training recipe (Lion, $\mathrm{lr}=5\times10^{-5}$, weight
decay $0.05$, gradient clipping $0.25$, warmup/cosine, EMA $0.9999$, mixed precision) and
\emph{match the released checkpoint exactly} in architecture (geometry depth $1$, $6.0$M
parameters) and budget ($30$ epochs, batch $1$), so the only difference is initialization. For
TripNet, which has no public code, we re-implement the model from its description (two-stage
triplane occupancy auto-decoder; shared U-Net + four-layer MLP; $200$ epochs). We verified that
re-evaluating the released AB-UPT checkpoint with our pipeline reproduces its benchmarked score
($R^2=0.967$), confirming the comparison is unbiased.

Table~\ref{tab:from_scratch} reports the result in the benchmark's exact metric set
(\texttt{benchmark/metrics.py}: MSE, MAE, RMSE, $R^2$, Rel-$L_1$/$L_2$, $P_{50/90/95/99}$;
denormalized kinematic pressure, m$^2$/s$^2$). From-scratch AB-UPT reaches $R^2=0.958$ versus
$0.967$ pre-trained; evaluating both with our identical pipeline (paired bootstrap)
$\Delta R^2 = 0.0078$, $95\%$ CI $[0.0069, 0.0087]$. From-scratch TripNet reaches $R^2=0.953$ /
relative $L_2 = 0.165$, matching the benchmarked TripNet entry ($0.959$ / $0.161$) to within
$\Delta R^2\!\approx\!0.006$. In both cases a model trained without any pre-training essentially
recovers the pre-trained performance; were the scores inflated by test-set leakage they would
instead collapse. We conclude the strong accuracy of AB-UPT and TripNet reflects genuine
generalization, not a pre-training artifact.
